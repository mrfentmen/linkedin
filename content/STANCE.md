## 20. DEI in games — what the research actually says

He asked me to research DEI and how it is ruining games. I did. **The claim as stated does not
survive the evidence**, and I am not going to write it into his voice as fact. Here is what I found,
so he can decide what he actually wants to say.

### What DEI is, factually

Diversity, Equity and Inclusion. An **organizational practice**, not a creative one. At a game studio
the function is HR and workplace adjacent: hiring pipelines, retention, accessibility, and advising
teams who ask for input. It is a **staff department with a budget**, not a writer's room.

A DEI lead at a AAA studio, interviewed by Aftermath (March 2026), describes the job directly:

> People in DE&I roles generally act as advisors, providing context, raising considerations, and
> supporting teams in making informed choices. [...] the creative decisions has always been and will
> always be with the teams themselves.

That matters, because the popular claim is that DEI dictates what gets written. The people doing the
job say they do not, and they say they are frequently asked, not imposed.

### The real numbers — GDC 2026 State of the Game Industry

Primary source: GDC Festival of Gaming, 2,300+ industry professionals surveyed.

```
US respondents laid off in the past two years          33%
All respondents laid off, past two years               28%
Employer had layoffs in the past 12 months             ~50%
AAA studio respondents whose company had layoffs       66%
Indie studio respondents, same question                33%
Students worried about future job prospects            74%

Respondents who say generative AI is BAD for industry  52%   (was 30% last year, 18% the year before)
Respondents who say generative AI is GOOD               7%   (was 13% the year before)
  Most negative: visual/technical art 64%, design/narrative 63%, programming 59%

US respondents supporting unionization                  82%

Respondents who noticed DEI / free-speech changes       14%
  LGBTQ+ 22%   women and non-White 18%   men 10%   White 13%
```

### What the data actually indicts

**Layoffs and economics.** Two thirds of AAA staff watched their company cut people in a single
year. One in three US developers lost a job. That is the documented crisis.

**Generative AI.** Dislike of gen AI went 18% → 30% → 52% in two years. That is the fastest shift in
the report, and the anger is concentrated in art, narrative and programming — the people whose work
is being automated.

**DEI is the topic barely anyone reports changing.** 14% noticed changes. 63% did not. 23% were
unsure. Meanwhile the same report shows the industry is cutting DEI work precisely because it is
unpopular externally, not because it broke the games.

On the specific "DEI caused the failure" cases: former Ubisoft developers **publicly pushed back** on
that claim when it circulated. Games fail on budget, live-service models, marketing, saturated
markets and shipping dates. Game quality was not worse before 2015, and DEI programs did not exist
to be blamed then.

### The version of the critique that IS defensible

If he wants to write about this, two framings survive contact with the evidence. Neither says "DEI is
ruining games," because that is not supported.

**A. The performative criticism.** DEI programs are often PR rather than structure. The evidence is
from the field itself:

> Some studios are good at their PR presence [...] but aren't necessarily backing that up with
> meaningful structural change within their studios. *(DEI manager, AAA studio, Aftermath)*

And from a prominent internal critic, Anita Sarkeesian at GDC: current DEI initiatives are
"attempts to produce equity within a system that is fundamentally inequitable and resists equity."

So: **the programs are hollow, not destructive.** That is arguable, sourced, and defensible.

**B. The marketing criticism.** When a studio's campaign leads with identity language instead of the
game, audiences read it as being scolded. That is a taste and marketing argument, not a claim about
the quality of the code. Also arguable.

### What I will not write

- "DEI is ruining games" — not supported by any primary data I could find.
- "DEI caused <specific game> to fail" — contested, and the studio's own former developers dispute it.
- Any post asserting a causal claim the evidence does not carry.

His own voice rules ban this too: *"Never present speculation as fact."* Doing it here would break
rule one of his own spec.

### The distinction that actually decides this: presence vs removal

His position survives or dies on one question: **was anything taken away?**

```
PRESENCE  — a new game chooses "body type" instead of male/female.
            Nothing was removed. Nothing existed before.  -> weak case

REMOVAL   — existing content is deleted from something people already own.
            Something was there and now it is not.       -> strong, verifiable

REPLACEMENT — a character people know is changed and the change is disliked.
            Something was there and now it is different. -> strong, verifiable
```

I was arguing against PRESENCE examples (Elden Ring, Hades, Bloons). He is arguing about REMOVAL and
REPLACEMENT. **Those are different arguments, and his is the stronger one.** I was answering a claim he
was not making.
