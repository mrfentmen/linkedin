What I did was send it back with a question. Which of these six still matter. Three were dropped in about ten minutes of conversation. Ten minutes. Two of the remaining ones turned out to be the same constraint written twice. Two constraints.

I now ask for that before I start anything older than a year. It takes about fifteen minutes and it has twice changed what the work actually was, once by about a week.

I ask which constraints still hold now. A ticket is a fossil and the fossil doesn't know it's out of date.

I spent about three hours last month on a bug that a colleague found in about two minutes by asking one question. Two minutes. Did this ever work.

I'd been reading the code and building a theory about what was wrong with it. He asked the question and I said it worked in January, and that answer narrowed it to about four commits, and the fix was in the third one.

What I'd skipped was the cheapest piece of information available. Not the state, not the logs. When it last worked, which tells you where to look and costs one conversation.

I use it as the first question now, before I open anything. When did this last work and what changed since. Four commits. It sounds obvious and I'd spent three hours not asking it.

I ask when it last worked now. It's one sentence and it's usually the whole search. Try it first.

A former colleague told me last week that he's spent about four months of this year on work that got cancelled. Four months. And he wasn't complaining. He said the four months taught him more than the two years before it.

The projects were killed for good reasons and they were big enough that he got to see the whole thing. Two were about a market that moved and one was about a bet on a technology that didn't land, and in all three cases the argument for stopping was in the original plan.

What I took from the conversation is that the value of a cancelled project depends on how early it got cancelled. His got stopped in month two, month four, and month six, and each one left behind a document the next team used.

I've been thinking about which of my own projects died in a way nobody learned from. Two of them, and both because I never wrote the reason down. Two projects. I write the reason down now.

I cancel early and write the reason now. A stopped project with a document is an asset and the same project without one is a story.
