A study with a small sample can still be worth reading. A study with a small sample presented as a large one is not.

I've read enough papers to have a habit and the habit is cheap. I find the number of participants before I read the conclusion, because the conclusion is written to sound complete and the sample size tells you how complete it can be.

There's a case I keep in mind. A sensitivity figure from a handful of patients got repeated in coverage as a general claim about a test. The paper didn't say that. The coverage did, and the coverage was built from the abstract. Nobody lied. Somebody compressed, and compression is how errors travel.

That's the failure mode I care about. Not fraud. Compression. A finding travels one step and loses its error bars on the way.

So my rule is to read the methods section before I repeat anything. It takes twenty minutes and it has stopped me from posting something wrong at least a dozen times this year.
