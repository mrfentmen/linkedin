The most useful thing I learned from physics wasn't a fact. It was a habit of asking what would change my mind.

Good experimental design has that built in. You state the result in advance and you state what would make it false. Otherwise the data gets interpreted to fit whatever you already believed.

I started doing the same thing with my own opinions about technology. What evidence would make me stop believing this. The answer is uncomfortable because I have to admit that nothing I have seen could do it.

That's the tell. If no possible observation would change your position, you aren't holding a belief, you're holding an identity. I have found this in my own work constantly. I was certain one tool was better until I wrote down what would prove me wrong, and then I noticed I had never tested the thing I was certain about.

Stating the test in advance is cheap. Skipping it costs years.
