It took me two years to learn to read a stack trace the right way and the trick is smaller than it sounds.

I used to read the top line, panic, and start guessing. The top line is where the program noticed. It's often four layers away from the line that caused it.

Now I read the bottom of my own code first. The last frame that belongs to us is the one that decided something wrong. Everything above that line is machinery doing what it was told.

I also read the whole trace before touching the keyboard. Ten seconds of reading beats ten minutes of guessing, every single time. I know that sounds like advice from a poster. It also happens to be the one change that cut my debugging time in half, and I found it because I stopped trusting my first guess. Read first. Type later. Guessing is fast. Being right is faster.
