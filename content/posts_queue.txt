A cron job scheduled at 2:30 in the morning runs twice a year or not at all, depending on the clock change. I found out from a duplicate invoice, which is the kind of bug nobody forgives.

Nothing in my code was wrong. The scheduler saw 2:30 twice on one night because the clocks went back, and it did what I told it to do both times.

Fixing it is about making the work safe to repeat, not about the schedule. My jobs now write a record when they start, keyed by the date they're meant to process. A second run sees that record and exits.

That one change protects me from clock changes, retries, a manual kick, and two schedulers running at once. All four have happened since.

If your job moves money or sends messages, make it safe to run twice. Times are less predictable than code. I add that record to every job I write now. Every one.
